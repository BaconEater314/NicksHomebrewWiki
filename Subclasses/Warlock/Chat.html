Warlock: Pact of the Chat

The Pact of the Chat, a Warlock who gains their powers from viewers who donate portions of their own magic to fuel them. Their viewers thrive on chaos, combat, awkward situations for the Warlock, able to view most every moment, should they choose to tune in.

This subclass is designed for all settings.

All Knowing Chat

When you choose this Pact, you gain access to the “all knowing” Chat that can help you out through the “casting” of certain spells. The following spells are added to your spell list, count as Warlock spells for you, and are always known to you.

Most of these spells have the Ritual tag on them. While Warlocks are not usually able to ritual cast, you can ritual cast these spells by getting information from your Chat as they culminate a final answer for you. However, for spells that may give garbled results after multiple casts, the cumulative chance is doubled for each casting after the first.

Chat, what Spell should I Cast?

Additionally, when you sign this Pact, you can choose to ask your chat which spell you can cast. Roll on the table of 1st level spells and cast that spell in the way of your choosing.

Alternatively, the Game Master may choose a spell you cast from the list, for better or for worse.

You gain access to higher level spell tables at higher levels, the 2nd level table at 3rd, the 3rd level table at 9th, the 4th level table at 13th, and the 5th level table at 17th.

If a spell requires material components with cost, consider it paid for via donation.

You can do this a number of times equal to your Charisma Modifier (minimum of 1). You gain all expended uses when you finish a long rest.

Chat, help me out rq.

Starting at 6th level, your viewership has grown enough that you can attempt to influence your polls. When you ask your Chat what spell you should cast, you can attempt to make a Persuasion check against a DC your GM decides. On a success, you can choose the spell you want to cast. On a failure, cannot attempt to ask Chat for help until the start of your next turn.

Once you successfully use this feature, you cannot use it again until you complete a short rest.

Mods, Ban this Man

Starting at 10th level, you have proper Moderators in your Chat. And with that, they hold power. You can cast the spell Banishment without concentration. Once you do so, you cannot do so again until you take a short rest.

Hype Train

Starting at 14th level, your success has allowed you to capitalize on your viewer’s hype via Hype Trains. Whenever you succeed on an attack roll, saving throw, or ability check, or at least one creature fails a saving throw against a spell you cast, you gain a stack of Hype Train. Scoring a Critical Hit gives you 2 stacks.

Consequently, failing an attack roll, saving throw, or ability check, or getting hit removes a stack of Hype Train. Rolling a 1 on an attack roll removes 2 stacks of Hype Train. You can have a minimum of 0 Hype Train stacks.

Hype Trains come in tiers. You gain higher tiers of the Hype Train by gaining stacks. You gain Tier 1 by having 3 stacks, Tier 2 with 6, Tier 3 with 12, Tier 4 with 24, and Tier 5 with 48. Your Hype Train ends whenever you spend 1 minute outside of combat or whenever you fall unconscious. However, if you ever roll a natural 20 on a Death Save while downed, you reignite your Hype Train to where it was when you fell.

Having Hype Train Tiers gives you the following benefits:

  • +1 AC per Hype Train Tier
  • +1 to all Spell Damage per Hype Train Tier
  • +1 to all Weapon Damage per Hype Train Tier
  • +5 Move Speed per Hype Train Tier
